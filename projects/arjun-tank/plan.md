# Plan v2 · Arjun: the tank waiting for its heart (for the owner's approval)

v2 applies the independent plan review (`review_plan_v1.md`: package 8/10, plan 7.5/10).

## The promise (**locked: the owner approved plan v2 on 2026-11-17; title left to us, thumbnail words WHERE'S THE ENGINE? chosen by the owner**)
**Promise in one sentence:** India built its own 68-tonne tank and in 2021 ordered 118 of the newest, due from 2024;
as far as anyone has reported, none has reached a regiment, because the one part India could not make in time is
the engine. You will see what that engine has to do, why the German factory stopped making it, what India
built instead in Mysuru, and the date that decides when the tanks roll.

**Title (main):** **India Ordered 118 Arjun Tanks. Where Are They?** (46 characters). A question, so it stays true
even if a handful arrive before upload (then sentence 1 changes, the title does not).
- Alt 1: 118 Arjun Tanks. One Missing Part. (35)
- Alt 2 (the search words): Why India's Arjun Tank Is Waiting for an Engine (47)
*Why:* the Tejas form that is our biggest film ("30 Tejas Jets Are Ready. Why Can't They Fly?"): a name everyone
knows, a number, a question only the film answers. "arjun mk1a" and "arjun tank" are what people type
(`projects/channel-research/2026-11-17/demand/arjun.json`). The owner's CTR rule (2026-11-17) applies: the title
can run a few characters long if a stronger one turns up, as long as it stays true.

**Thumbnail concept (main): "NO ENGINE"**: bright Thar daylight, warm low sun, sand. An Arjun from the rear
three-quarter, big in frame, its rear engine deck cut open: the engine bay is **empty**, lit from inside in caution
yellow, with the bolt holes and pipe ends where the engine should sit. Words, upper left, caution yellow on a dark
band: **WHERE'S THE ENGINE?** (a question, true whatever the delivery count; the review's pick over NO ENGINE,
which a commuter can read as clickbait and which turns false if one tank arrives). Bottom-right empty. Visual question: *a tank with a hole where its heart should be?* The title
gives the number and the name; the picture gives the reason; the words never repeat the title.
- Challenger 0 (kept for Test & Compare): **NO ENGINE**, same picture.
- Challenger 1: **WAITING SINCE 2021**: the same tank, the bay closed, a line of tank silhouettes behind it fading
  into haze (118 as ghost outlines, not real hulls), the words in signal red.
- Challenger 2: **1,500 HP**: the plain-object version: India's own engine glowing on a steel test bed, a crane
  hook above it, the tank's empty bay waiting below.
- Not used: a German flag (blame, not true: MTU is a company with an order book); "INDIA CAN'T" (not true: the
  engine ran in Mysuru); a T-90 beside it (wins the mockers' click, loses the story).
- Picture source: our own Blender Arjun (built from real reference photos), or an AI picture from our render as
  reference, as for the Matsya and carrier thumbnails. No real person.

**Sentence 1 (promise check, draft):** "On the fourteenth of February, twenty twenty-one, in Chennai, the Prime Minister
handed India's newest tank to the Army. Seven months later, India ordered one hundred and eighteen of them. The
first were due in twenty twenty-four. As far as anyone has reported, not one has reached a regiment. The tank is
built. What it is waiting for is its engine."
Spoken slower than v1 (one number per sentence, for viewers on subtitles). It confirms all variants: the empty
bay (WHERE'S THE ENGINE?), the wait (WAITING SINCE 2021) and, by 0:40, the engine
India built (1,500 HP). It never says India cannot build engines.

**Edge:** every Arjun film argues whether it is a good tank. In 2026 the tank is not the problem: 118 are ordered and
ordered, and they wait for a heart, a 1,400-horsepower diesel a German factory stopped making, while India's own
1,500-horsepower engine is still proving itself between a test bed in Mysuru and a test vehicle in Avadi.

**Honesty rules for this film:**
- "None delivered" is always attributed and dated ("as of January 2026, reports say"; re-searched within 24 h of
  upload). If one has been delivered by then, the opening changes and the promise check re-runs.
- MTU is a company that stopped a line for lack of orders, said so without blame; no "Germany betrayed India".
- The 2010 trials were run by the Army; the result is said as "reported results, never published in full" (fair
  to both the Army and DRDO). The Army's doubts are said in its own words (Gen Deepak Kapoor
  2008, Maj Gen A.P. Singh), and the Army's own 2021 order is the answer.
- 69 per cent imported **at induction** is the CAG's figure, said plainly, with what is reported about Indian
  content now, and India's honour is defended with what
  changed since, not by hiding it.
- DATRAN is "being proven", never "ready": its milestones only (first fired March 2024, test vehicle December 2025).
- The weight is not hidden: it is a desert tank by design, and the weight grew with what the Army asked for
  (Mk1 about 58.5 t, Mk1A about 68 t): a shared decision, not DRDO's alone.
- The engine and the gearbox are both German (MTU engine, Renk transmission); DATRAN replaces both (engine + its own
  1,500 hp automatic transmission). The film says so.
- The Army chose to wait for India's engine rather than take a few tanks with the small stock of old engines (idrw,
  attributed): said, because it changes "stuck" into "chose".
- "Kartavya Path", never "Rajpath".

**Length:** about 13 minutes. **Feeling curve:** pride (open) → puzzlement (ch1) → wonder (ch2) → hurt (ch3) →
honesty, a little uncomfortable (ch4) → understanding (ch5) → resolve (ch6) → steady hope (landing). No two
neighbours share a feeling.

## 2. Cold opens considered
The obvious one, set aside: drone shots of tanks firing with "the Arjun is India's pride".
1. **The handover (chosen): one moment.** 14 Feb 2021, Chennai: the real DD / PIB footage of the Mk1A rolling past
   the stage. Sentence 1. Then the camera moves round to the rear deck, the deck opens in our cutaway, and the bay
   is empty and glowing (the thumbnail). *Why:* first frame = the thumbnail's subject, a real day many Indians saw
   on the news, turned into a question.
2. **The viewer's own life.** "In twenty twenty-one, maybe you booked a car and the dealer said: waiting period."
   Warm and shared (the chip-shortage waits), but the first frame is not a tank; kept for ch1 as the comparison
   (if sourced in step 4).

   on scorn, not pride.

## 3. Endings considered
1. **The bay fills (chosen).** Back to the tank from the opening. The empty glowing bay; a crane lowers India's own

## 6. Beat sheet (about 13:00)
### Cold open (0:00–0:45)
- 0:00 Q: what happened to this tank? Picture: the Chennai handover (real), the rear deck, our cutaway opening to
  the empty glowing bay. Sound: crowd, then wind. Music: none until 0:30.
- 0:20 Turn: "118 ordered… not one has reached a regiment". The engine-start whine begins and does not catch.
- 0:35 Open loop: "India did build an engine. It roared in Mysuru two years ago. So why are the tanks still
  waiting? And why does it matter so much what sits in this hole?" Music in.

### Ch1 · The order (0:45–2:15) · puzzlement · device: the viewer's own wait (one sentence only)
- Q: what exactly was promised? 23 Sep 2021, HVF Avadi, Rs 7,523 crore, 200 vendors, five in 30 months then 30 a
  year (Janes). The car-booking waiting period (if sourced). Turn: Feb 2024, a DRDO official to Janes: an engine
  shortage. By 2:00: "You have read the comments under every Arjun video: too heavy,
  desert only, German engine. Hold on to them. We will answer each one." Where are they, plainly: what is reported
  about the hulls at Avadi; the Army chose to wait for India's engine rather than take a few with old stock.
  Payoff: the tank's only missing part is the one that makes it a tank. Picture: the HVF gate at Avadi,
  Chennai, real factory footage; the calendar of promised deliveries emptying.

### Ch2 · What 1,400 horses must do (2:15–4:30) · wonder · device: the human scale
- Q: why can't any engine do? 68 tonnes at desert heat through sand. The engine as Swift engines (about sixteen,
  OUR CALCULATION, if sourced); the heat it must throw away; the dust it breathes; the powerpack lifted out as one
  block. Turn: power-to-weight about 20 hp per tonne, and every horsepower makes heat that the Thar will not take.
  The question posed here, not at 11:00: "Why not just buy another engine?" The answer is the payoff: an engine
  for a tank is a whole system (engine, gearbox, cooling, filters) shaped to one hull; the gearbox is German too
  (Renk); you cannot just drop another one in.
  Hero 3D: the powerpack in the bay, air and heat shown flowing.

### Ch3 · Fifty-two years (4:30–5:50, cut to about 80 s) · hurt · device: one object followed through time
- Q: is the Arjun really ours? 1974, Avadi; the first tanks to the Army 2004-05; 69 per cent imported at induction
  (CAG). Then the CAG's sentence: not operational since 2013, for want of spares. April 2015: the Ministry orders
  20 tanks made fit by August. The 2010 Mahajan trials: one sentence, "reported results". Turn: the Army still ordered 118 in 2021.
  Payoff: the hard truth and the honour together: India learned to build the body; the parts it imported were the
  parts that stopped it. Picture: the bay through the years; the CAG page on the drafting table (the one document
  pose).

### Ch4 · Built for the desert (5:50–7:40) · honesty · device: the map
- Q: "too heavy" (the top comment). 68 tonnes against Punjab bridges built for about 50; the T-90 at 48; the rail
  platform story (Maj Gen A.P. Singh). Turn: it was built for the Rajasthan plains, and there it is good (the 2021
  order). Answers the three top comments by name: mud and the east, desert only, "why not T-90s" (a fair answer: India has
  about 1,500 T-90S, lighter, a Russian design built under licence, a different job). The Army asked for the weight.
  Payoff: a heavy tank for the desert, by design; Zorawar for Ladakh in one line. Map: India's official boundary.

### Ch5 · A factory's order book (7:40–9:40) · understanding · device: the other side, then the world, then the choice
- Q: why won't Germany just make it? MTU's line for this engine stopped; a restart needs a bigger order and about
  four years (Janes 2024; idrw 2026: not for about 120 engines). Turn: no villain, a business decision; the risk was
  always in depending on one foreign line. Then the world (new to every Arjun film): Turkey's Altay waited years when Germany would not
  export its MTU/Renk powerpack (over Syria) and went Korean; Korea's own K2 transmission failed durability tests
  six times and Korea bought a German one. **Then the choice (fact-check, C17):** in November 2025 Rolls-Royce, which owns MTU, reportedly
  offered to make the same engine in India with full transfer of technology, awaiting the Ministry's clearance
  (none reported since). So India is choosing between the old German engine made at home and its own. Payoff: tank
  hearts are hard for everyone; the risk is depending on one
  foreign line, and the cure is your own, proven over thousands of kilometres.

### Ch6 · The engine India built (9:40–11:40) · resolve · device: the Kishore moment
- "You might ask me, Kishore: same story as Tejas? We can't make engines?" The answer: Mysuru, 20 Mar 2024, the
  1,500 hp engine fires (PIB); "Generation One" done; 4 Dec 2025, the DATRAN test vehicle at Avadi; the Cummins
  stop-gap for old tanks (if sourced). Turn: an engine that runs on a bed is not an engine that runs a tank across
  the Thar for years. One line on what comes next: the future tank (FRCV) will need this engine too, so it decides the
  next thirty years. Payoff: **one** date to watch: the DATRAN test vehicle's mobility trials (to be dated in
  step 4), then the Army's own trials.

### Landing (11:40–12:50) · steady hope
- The bookend: the empty bay, the crane, DATRAN lowered, stopping above the mounts. The saying. One date to watch. Subscribe line, one question ("Would you wait for India's own engine, or buy a foreign one to get the tanks
  now?"), the next film as a question, "This is Be Practical with Kishore. Stay practical."

## 8a. Fact-check results (step 4, 2026-11-17): `claims.csv` passes the gate (75 rows: 43 confirmed, 23 claim, 4 disputed, 5 our calculation)
The checker worked in a fresh context. What changes in the narration:
- **The 2008 "no further orders" line is Lt Gen Dalip Bharadwaj's** (DG Mechanised Forces, Business Standard and
  domain-b, July 2008), not Gen Deepak Kapoor's; Kapoor's "mid-level technology" only via The Wire 2021, attributed
  or dropped.
- **The 2010 trials are public through the CAG (Report 35 of 2014), quoting the Army's own report:** overall T-90
  75.01, Arjun 72.46 (the gap mostly missile firing, which Arjun was not built for); Arjun marginally better in
  accuracy and consistency of fire; the Army: Arjun "had performed creditably"; the CAG found stricter tests set for
  Arjun in eight cases. Ch3 uses this, not "reported results" and never "beat the T-90".
- **Mk1 about 62 t** (the Army to a Parliament committee), Mk1A about 68 t; T-90 "under fifty tonnes"; "more than a
  thousand T-90s"; no T-72 weight.
- **About seventeen Swift engines** (60 kW today, Maruti), about 20 hp per tonne: both OUR CALCULATION.
- **1974:** May 1974; cost dropped (sources give 15.5 and 16 crore).
- **Altay:** the German government's export restrictions (Syria), not MTU or Renk refusing; deliveries announced
  24 Feb 2025.
- **DATRAN** is, in DRDO's words, the 1,500 hp transmission: say "India's own engine and gearbox". Programme
  director S. Srinivasa Rao.
- **Cut:** the Cummins stop-gap, "MTU quit spares", the Rs 1,200 crore parts story. Use instead: AVNL's Engine
  Factory Avadi repaired two 1,400 hp Arjun engines in FY2024-25 (primary).
- **Mysuru:** "first official test-firing", 20 March 2024 (PIB); an earlier 2023 run is reported. The project
  missed its own "mid-2025" completion target (PIB 2024).
- **New for ch5:** Rolls-Royce (MTU's owner since 2021, "Rolls-Royce Solutions") reportedly offered in Nov 2025 to
  make the MB 838 in India with full transfer of technology, awaiting MoD clearance; none reported since. MTU also
  reportedly offered a newer engine needing years of trials. The factory stays blameless.
- **Attributions:** "none delivered" = "as of January 2026, reports say; none reported since" (no Parliament, MoD,
  Army or AVNL statement found to 17 Nov 2026; AVNL's FY2024-25 report lists none); "at least four more years" = a
  DRDO official to Janes; "the Army chose to wait" = reported in 2025, and in Feb 2026 two engines were reported
  under evaluation (an MTU-based CVRDE engine and DATRAN); bridges, the rail platform and "boutique tank" = The Wire
  and Maj Gen A.P. Singh; 54.3% Indian content (2021) = reported.
- **The date to watch has no official date:** reports range from late 2025–2027 to "not before 2029". The landing
  says "the test vehicle's trials, and the Ministry's decision on the Rolls-Royce offer", without a year.
- **Cold open:** check the handover footage: one caption says the PM handed over a replica; PIB's "handed over" is
  safe either way, and the picture must show the real tank.
