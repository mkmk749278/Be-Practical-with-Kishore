  **breakthrough film with an honest wound**, and not an engine. Roadmap #19 (reactor pressure tubes).
- **The machine:** the pressurised heavy water reactor (PHWR). It has no single pressure vessel. Instead, a few hundred
  horizontal zirconium-niobium **pressure tubes** (coolant channels) run through a tank of cold heavy water (the
  calandria). Each tube carries hot, high-pressure heavy water past the fuel bundles, under heat, pressure and
  neutron radiation for decades. The tubes creep, sag, pick up hydrogen and can crack. They are the reactor's single
  point of failure.
- **The paradox (working):** after Pokhran in 1974, Canada walked out of the Rawatbhata reactors and the world closed
  its nuclear doors to India. The refusal should have stopped India's nuclear power. Instead India built a reactor that
  runs on its own natural uranium, scaled it from 220 MW to 700 MW, and learned to replace every pressure tube inside
  a radioactive core by remote control.
- **The live hook:** the regulator (AERB) cleared RAPP-8 at Rawatbhata, Rajasthan, for first criticality on
  19 NOV 2026 (PIB, 20 NOV): the 4th indigenous 700 MW unit.
- **The honest wound:** the coolant-channel leaks at Kakrapar, Gujarat (2015, 2016), years of shutdown, delays and
  cost overruns on the 700 MW fleet, nuclear's small share of India's power.
- **Who it's for:** the men over 45 who remember Pokhran (1974 and 1998) and the sanctions; the viewers of the big
  Indian nuclear films (KK Create 6.6M, Mohak Mangal 1.7M, Apoorv 1.2M) who were told about thorium but never shown
  the reactor that actually carries the load; the diaspora who hear "India's nuclear power is Russian or French".
