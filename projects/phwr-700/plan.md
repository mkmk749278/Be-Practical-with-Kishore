# Plan v1 · India's own nuclear reactor: the one Canada walked out of (for review, then the owner)

Research: `research/landscape.md` (two passes, 23 NOV 2026), `claims.csv` (31 draft rows). Every fact below has a
claims row or is marked **[to source]**; nothing goes into the narration until `claims_check.py` passes.

## 1. Angles that differ in kind (chosen: C)
- **A · The record:** Kaiga-1 ran 962 days without stopping, a world record. Proud, but a scoreboard: no wound, no
  machine and no stakes. Swap test: it could be any power plant.
- **B · The scale-up:** from 220 MW to 700 MW, the fleet of ten, 100 GW by 2047. A ministry brochure. Viewers scroll
  past (Matsya).
- **C · Two refusals and a repair (chosen):** in 1974 Canada walked out of a half-built reactor in Rajasthan. By 1995
  the tubes inside India's reactors were ageing, and Canada's rules kept its experts from helping fix them. India
  decided to do it "with or without outside help", and in 1996–97 pulled every tube out of a radioactive core by
  remote control: "only the second time in the world" (NPCIL). That skill is why India can build its own 700 MW
  reactors today. *Why C:* it has a machine under extreme heat and pressure (our formula), a refusal every older
  Indian remembers (Pokhran), an honest wound (Kakrapar 2016), real Indian people at work, and a win the viewer
  can feel without chest-thumping. Nobody on YouTube tells it (`landscape.md` §1).
- D · Thorium hype vs reality: crowded with 1M-view films; folded in as one objection.

## 2. The promise (to lock with the owner)
**Promise in one sentence:** in 1974 Canada walked out of India's first heavy-water reactor and the world shut its
nuclear doors. You'll see the 392 thin tubes that are the real heart of India's own reactors, why they crack, and how
Indian engineers learned to replace every one of them inside a radioactive core, so that this month India could start
its fourth 700 MW reactor of its own design.

**Title (main):** **Canada Walked Out of India's Nuclear Reactor** (44). A household name, the contradiction, and the
search phrase "india nuclear reactor"; the film answers "so what happened?". True as written (C02, C03, C26).
- Alt 1 (name, number, contradiction): **1974: Canada Left. India Built a 700 MW Reactor** (45)
- Alt 2 (search-first): **How India Built Its Own Nuclear Reactor Alone** — *rejected:* "alone" is untrue (RAPS-1 was
  Canadian; fuel is imported today). Replaced by: **India's Own Nuclear Reactor: Built After They Left** (49, check
  length with the owner's CTR rule).

**Thumbnail concept (main): "THEY LEFT."** The face of a PHWR (our Blender render, warm workshop light, half the frame
lit): a wall of hundreds of round steel end fittings in a grid, one fitting open, and **one long glowing tube drawn
halfway out** across the frame, held by a yellow remote-handling arm. Words, upper left, caution yellow on a dark band:
**THEY LEFT.** Bottom-right empty. Visual question: *who left, and who is pulling the heart out of this machine?*
The title names Canada and the reactor; the picture shows the mystery object; the words carry the feeling. Every word
is paid off by 0:20.
- Challenger 1: **392 TUBES**: the same face lit from inside, one tube glowing red, a crack line on it.
- Challenger 2 (the bright plain object): the Rawatbhata domes at sunset beside the lake (real photo if licensed, else
  render), one new dome lit; words **OUR OWN** in safety orange.
- Challenger 3: **1974**, in signal red, over the empty half-built reactor hall (illustration, labelled in the film).

## 3. Cold opens (each a different kind; judged against the promise)
1. **The refusal (chosen).** First frame: the reactor face from the thumbnail, the tube drawn out, then a match cut to
   1974. Sentence 1: "In nineteen seventy four, Canada walked out of a half built nuclear reactor in the Rajasthan
   desert." Then: "Last week, on the same ground, India was cleared to start its fourth seven hundred megawatt reactor.
   Its own design." Paradox by 0:25: "A country the world shut out now builds its own. How?" Open loop by 0:45: "The
   answer isn't a bomb or a budget. It's a tube ten centimetres wide. By the end you'll know why India learned a
   repair only one other country had ever done." *Confirms the title in sentence 1 and the thumbnail by 0:20.*
2. *The failure:* 11 MAR 2016, about 9 am, Kakrapar: the reactor shuts itself down; heavy water is leaking from one
   tube, and it takes eleven days to stop. Gripping, but it opens on a leak and promises a safety film, not the title.
   Kept as the chapter 4 set piece's alternative, not used.
3. *The object under stress:* "Right now, inside a reactor in Rajasthan, there's a tube as wide as a steel tumbler
   holding water at three hundred and ten degrees…" Strong image, but sentence 1 doesn't confirm "Canada walked out".
   Its image moves to chapter 1.
4. *The viewer's home:* "If your fan is running in Jaipur or Delhi tonight…" Lands well at the end; too soft to open.

## 4. Endings (each a different kind)
1. **The circle closes (chosen):** March 2026, Canada's biggest uranium company signs a deal to sell India fuel for
   nine years (C19). The country that walked out now sells us fuel for the reactor we learned to build and repair
   ourselves. Then the viewer's fan, the saying, the date.
2. *The record:* Kaiga-1's 962 days. A scoreboard; it goes into chapter 4 instead.
3. *The future:* 100 GW by 2047 and the fleet of ten. It becomes the yardstick in the landing, not the last image.

## 5. The creative spine
- **The one image:** **392 pressure cookers.** A home cooker whistles at about 1 kg/cm² **[to source: manufacturer
  manual]**; each tube holds about 100 kg/cm² at 310 °C (C08). So the reactor face becomes 392 cookers, each pushing
  a hundred times harder than the one in your kitchen, with walls about 4 mm thick. Sized from real numbers, from
  every Indian home.
- **The motif: the reactor face.** A grid of circles that returns five times and means more each time: (1) the cold
  open, one tube drawn out; (2) ch.1, every circle a pressure cooker; (3) ch.3, one circle goes dark (Pickering 1983);
  (4) ch.4, the circles go out one by one and come back new (RAPS-2, 306 of them); (5) the end, 392 circles light up at
  RAPP-8. Sound: the deep clunk of a fuelling machine locking onto a tube, every time the face returns.
- **Feeling curve:** wonder (ch.1) → abandoned (ch.2) → dread (ch.3) → pride (ch.4) → resolve (ch.5) → warmth (landing).
- **One device per chapter:** ch.1 one object followed through (a fuel bundle's journey); ch.2 then-and-now (the same
  Rawatbhata ground, 1974 and 2026); ch.3 a withheld answer (what broke the Canadian tube?); ch.4 a reversal (the
  country that couldn't get help becomes the one offering the repair, NPCIL's export claim, attributed); ch.5 a
  confession (Kishore: "I'll be honest with you: nuclear is three percent of our power").
- **The retell line:** "Canada walked out of our reactor in 1974. Twenty years later, India was pulling every tube
  out of a radioactive reactor by remote control, something only one other country had done."
- **The saying:** **"What you can repair, you truly own."** (climax of ch.4 and the landing)
- **Signature moments:** (1) the 392-cooker face; (2) the ring that slid a metre (Pickering): a bangle-sized garter
  spring creeps along a hot tube until hot touches cold and the crack opens, in slow motion; (3) the remote tool
  drawing a whole tube out of the reactor face while the engineers watch on screens.
- **Art direction:** warm steel and Rajasthan sandstone, sodium-orange workshop light, the cold blue of heavy water
  only inside the calandria. **Sound palette:** the fuelling-machine clunk, a pressure-cooker whistle (once, ch.1),
  wind on the Rawatbhata site, the hiss of a leak (ch.3, ch.4), a low tanpura drone under the landing.
