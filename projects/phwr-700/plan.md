## 2. The promise (locked 23 NOV, see §0)
**Promise:** in 1974 Canada walked out of a half-built nuclear reactor in Rajasthan. India finished it. Then its 306
tubes began to wear out, and the only country that had ever replaced them all was Canada, whose rules wouldn't let it
help. You'll see how Indian engineers rebuilt the heart of that same reactor by remote control, what it taught them,
and why it matters for the 700 MW reactor India was cleared to start in November 2026.

## 5. The creative spine
- **The one image: one big pot, or 306 pressure cookers?** Most reactors are one giant steel pot. India's is 306 small
  pressure vessels (tubes) running through a tank. Each holds about 100 kg/cm² at 310 °C (C08), a hundred times the
  push of the cooker in your kitchen (C32), with walls about 4 mm thick. *Why tubes and not one pot:* natural uranium
  and no giant forgings **[to source: NPCIL/BARC on why PHWR]**.
- **The motif: the reactor face.** It returns five times: (1) the cold open, the flask locked on; (2) ch.1, 306
  circles become 306 cookers; (3) ch.2, the camera pushes in until one circle fills the frame, then the crack (Pickering
  1983); (4) ch.3, the circles go dark one by one and come back new; (5) the end, the face grows from 306 to 392 (RAPP-8).
  The sound is the deep clunk of the flask locking on, always between sentences.
- **The feeling curve:** pride and friendship (ch.1 open) → abandoned (ch.1 end) → dread (ch.2) → grit (ch.3) →
  honesty (ch.4) → resolve (ch.5) → warmth (landing).
- **One device per chapter:**
  - ch.1: then-and-now on the same ground.
  - ch.2: a withheld answer (what killed the Canadian tube?).
  - ch.3: one object followed through (one old tube, from cut to flask to vault).
  - ch.4: a plain confession of a failure.
  - ch.5: the honest arithmetic (OUR CALCULATION).
- **The retell line:** "Canada walked out in 1974. When the tubes wore out, only Canada knew how to change them, and it
  wasn't allowed to help. So India learned alone, on the same reactor."
- **The saying:** our fathers never threw away a broken radio or scooter. They opened it. **"A machine is truly yours
  only when you can open it and mend it."** (the climax of ch.3 and the landing)
- **Signature moments:**
  1. One pot vs 306 cookers.
  2. The ring that slid: a coiled-spring spacer ring, about as wide as a katori, creeps along a hot tube until hot touches
     cold. Hydrogen gathers there like dew on a cold glass, and the crack opens. **[to source: the "one metre", and the
     dew analogy checked against the physics of hydride migration]**
  3. The old tube drawn into the shielded flask while the engineers watch on screens (real footage if found).
- **The look:** warm steel, Rajasthan sandstone, the green of the Chambal and the lake. Plain steel, nothing glows. The
  cold blue of heavy water only inside the tank.
- **The sound:** the clunk, river and birds at Rawatbhata, the hum of pumps, one cooker whistle (ch.1), a tanpura under
  the landing. Every effect sits between sentences, and the music drops under speech.
