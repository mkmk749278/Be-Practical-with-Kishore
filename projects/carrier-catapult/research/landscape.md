### 8.2 The 1971 story holds, and is better than the first pass knew
Source chain: Vice Admiral G. M. Hiranandani, *Transition to Triumph* (the Navy's official history, 1965-75), quoted
by livehistoryindia.com and by a Navy veteran's blog (V. Karve, 2020); Admiral Arun Prakash in The Wire. The book
itself is still to read (**Cowork request**: indiannavy.nic.in history pages).
- Mid-1970: cracks in the water drum of boiler A1; Hiranandani: "many internal fatigue cracks and fissures in the
  water drums of her boilers and these could not be repaired by welding". New drums were "beyond indigenous
  capability at that time". Speed limited to 14 knots.
- Arun Prakash: "High-pressure steam from the boilers not only drove the ship's engines, but also the 'catapult'
  that launched aircraft." "If pushed too hard in their precarious condition, they could simply blow up." "After
  much agonising, NHQ decided that Vikrant, with all its limitations, would go to war."
- Admiral Nanda: "If the same thing happened in 1971, Vikrant would be called a white elephant and naval aviation
  would be written off."
- **The person:** Commander Benoy Roy Chowdhury ("Bilu"), Vikrant's Engineer Officer. His team put **steel bands
  round the boiler**, adjusted "the safety valves to accept greater risk", left the boiler room unmanned or sparsely
  manned and worked the oil valve from near the upper deck. A burst drum would have filled the machinery spaces with
  superheated steam. He received the **Vir Chakra**, "the first Engineering Officer to be honoured with a VrC".
- 4 December 1971, about 3 pm: six Sea Hawks launched for Cox's Bazar (Rear Admiral S. K. Gupta's recollection).
  Only six Sea Hawks were available when training began in July 1971.
- *The "embargo" and "steam re-routed from the forward machinery" details rest on a fan wiki only: dropped unless
  the book has them.*
- 1987-89 refit: ramp fitted (about 9.75°, sources differ), catapult removed (navypedia; naval-encyclopedia).
  *Official history "Transition to Eminence" to check.*

### 8.3 The ski-jump penalty: no official number exists
- Nobody official has published what a MiG-29K or Rafale M can lift off Vikrant. Estimates: Rafale M about 20 to 21
  tonnes off a ramp (photo analysis, meta-defense 2023) against a 24.5-tonne maximum; CSIS puts a J-15 at about
  62,000 lb off Liaoning's ramp. A Fujian provincial government page quotes a Chinese official: the ski-jump "has
  many restrictions" on weight, so jets "can only bring certain amounts of fuel and weapons".
- Air Marshal M. Matheswaran (Lowy, 2020): ski-jump carriers are "handicapped by their inability to launch heavier
  platforms" such as airborne early warning aircraft; Vikramaditya spends "at least 70%" of its resources on its own
  air defence.
- **So the film says it honestly:** the Navy has never published the number; the direction is not in doubt; and the
  loss nobody disputes is the radar aircraft. China's KJ-600 "is restricted to catapult launches due to its size and
  weight" (USNI, Sept 2025) and flew from Fujian that month.

### 8.4 The number that becomes a thing (OUR CALCULATION, to settle in step 4)
- One launch delivers up to about 122 MJ; 121 MJ is about **34 units of electricity** (kWh). A 30-tonne jet at
  130 knots carries about 67 MJ, about 19 units. At ordinary home tariffs that is **roughly ₹150 to ₹250 of
  electricity**, about what a small home uses in ten days.
- The same energy in 2 to 3 seconds is about 50 MW on average and near 100 MW at the peak (force × speed at the end
  of the stroke): more than all four of Vikrant's gas turbines together (80 to 88 MW), about the evening demand of a
  town of five lakh people. **The energy is small. The three seconds are the problem.**
- That is why the ship fills four spinning disks for 45 seconds and empties them in three.
- A gas-turbine ship like Vikrant has no steam plant at all, so "just fit steam" would mean adding boilers (the
  same reason Britain's carriers have ramps; NAO report to cite).
- DRDO: 400 kg over 16 to 18 m. The Navy's need: 30 to 40 tonnes over about 90 m: **75 to 100 times the mass.**

### 8.6 The objection ledger, final (from the comments above)
1. **"Who is stopping India from developing its own?"** Nobody. DRDO has: 400 kg. The gap is 75 to 100 times, a
   decade or more by its own officials, and there is no approved ship to put it in. (ch5, the Kishore moment)
2. **"America offered it and can't be trusted / just buy it."** Reports of an offer in 2017, never a signed sale;
   the price France was quoted is $1.321 billion for one ship set; the ship and its power plant are the bigger
   missing parts. (ch5)
3. **"Did nobody measure the lift? How much less does it carry?"** The lift as reported; the payload number has never
   been published; the sure loss is the radar aircraft. (ch1)
4. **"Even America is going back to steam, so it's a failed technology."** 614 against 4,166, and 36,863 launches;
   a plan was ordered for one ship; and Vikrant has no steam to go back to. (ch4)
5. **"China did it."** Yes: Fujian, commissioned 5 Nov 2025, launched a radar aircraft in Sept 2025. Said plainly.
   (ch4)
6. **"Our islands are unsinkable carriers; build drones and submarines instead."** A real argument inside India's
   own forces (General Rawat, 2020). One fair line; the full answer goes in the pinned comment. (landing)
